## 1. Mortality awareness: when it motivates vs. paralyzes
**How** death is contemplated matters more than **whether**.
- **Terror Management Theory (TMT):** death reminders trigger anxiety/defensive
  reactions ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terror_management_theory)).
  **Credibility flag:** the classic "mortality salience → worldview defense" effect
  **largely failed to replicate** — Many Labs 4 (17 labs, 1,550 ppl) found ≈0 (d≈0.03)
  vs the original d≈0.5 ([Collabra](https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/8/1/35271/168050/),
  [COS](https://www.cos.io/blog/many-labs-4-failure-replicate-mortality-salience-effect-and-without-original-author-involvement));
  a 2023 PLOS One paper agreed ([PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0285267)).
  Treat "death reminders backfire" claims with caution.
- **Death reflection:** deliberate, personalized, meaning-focused contemplation
  *increases* gratitude, intrinsic goals, prosocial behavior ([Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1507212/full)).
- **Cozzolino's dual-existential-systems model** (best reconciler / strongest anchor):
  *abstract, impersonal* death primes → defense/anxiety; *specific, personalized,
  reflective* engagement → intrinsic values and generosity, especially for otherwise
  materialistic people ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21742931/)).

→ Frame the grid as personal **reflection and meaning-making**, not abstract doom. Pair
finitude with agency and self-esteem buffers (self-esteem reduces death defensiveness,
[ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886921000878)).

## 2. Goal-setting & follow-through
- **Locke & Latham:** specific, challenging goals beat "do your best" — **but only while
  perceived attainable** (difficulty↔performance r≈0.82, collapsing to r≈0.11 for
  impossible goals). Commitment + feedback required ([MindTools](https://www.mindtools.com/azazlu3/lockes-goal-setting-theory/)).
- **Gollwitzer's implementation intentions** ("if [situation], then I will [behavior]"):
  highest-leverage follow-through tool — d≈0.65 across 94 studies/8,000+ ppl; bigger with
  true if-then format, high motivation, and one rehearsal ([642-test meta-analysis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563)).
- **Why people fail to plan:** present bias / hyperbolic discounting + **future-self
  disconnection** — the brain treats the future self like a *stranger*; vivid, concrete
  future representations reduce discounting ([Hershfield, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3949005/)).
  Time-blocking productivity multipliers ("25%/2×") are vendor-cited, **low confidence**.

## 3. Gamification without dread
Use **Self-Determination Theory** (autonomy, competence, relatedness) as the test.
Need-satisfying gamification drives intrinsic motivation; pressure-inducing gamification
erodes it ([Springer meta-analysis 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7);
[NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/autonomy-relatedness-competence/)). Gamification
reliably boosts autonomy/relatedness but has *minimal* effect on competence — don't
over-rely on points/badges. **Streaks** can flip from competence signal to loss-aversion
pressure → offer freezes/forgiveness; frame progress positively.

## 4. Onboarding an emotionally heavy concept gently
Build trust **before** asking for vulnerability; prioritize cognitive ease (short
intro); warm/optimistic copy; celebrate small wins; make psychological safety the
explicit goal ([UX Magazine](https://uxmag.com/articles/the-psychology-of-onboarding-first-impressions-rule-the-brain)).
_(Conversion percentages are practitioner case-study figures — directional, not
peer-reviewed.)_

## 5. Tim Urban's framework (accurate)
- **Procrastination:** Rational Decision-Maker vs. Instant Gratification Monkey vs.
  Panic Monster (wakes only at deadlines, the only thing the Monkey fears). Key warning:
  the *dangerous* procrastination is **deadline-free** life goals — no Panic Monster ever
  triggers ([TED talk](https://thespeakerlab.com/blog/tim-urban-ted-talk/)).
- **"Your Life in Weeks" + "The Tail End":** ~4,680-week grid; by HS graduation you've
  used ~93% of in-person time with your parents. Prioritization through finitude, not
  despair ([WBW wiki](https://wait-but-why.fandom.com/wiki/The_Tail_End)).

## Design principles for the product
1. **Reflection, not dread** — specific, personalized, agency-paired framing; always
   show "weeks ahead + what you can do."
2. **The grid is a future-self bridge** — let users place concrete future events/goals
   on specific weeks.
3. **Convert aspirations into if-then plans** — the single highest-leverage feature.
4. **Goals: specific + challenging + attainable** — give progress feedback.
5. **Gentle, trust-first onboarding** — values first, full grid reveal after a warm
   ~3-step intro; defer the heaviest "weeks remaining" view.
6. **SDT-aligned gamification** — reward autonomy/relatedness; forgiving streaks.
7. **Give finitude a counterweight** — pair confronting views with self-esteem buffers +
   a clear next action.
8. **Off-ramps and tone control** — calm design, opt-in intensity, easy exits.

**Confidence:** TMT-backfire caveat = high. Time-blocking multipliers & onboarding
percentages = low-moderate. Cozzolino, Gollwitzer, Hershfield, SDT, Urban framework =
high.
